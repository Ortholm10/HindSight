# L04 — Higher-timeframe merge without closure lag

**Looks like.** A daily strategy pulls in a weekly or 4-hour indicator merged on
a timestamp falling *inside* the higher-timeframe bar. The HTF value at row `t`
includes bars that had not closed at `t`. Inflation is modest and plausible —
Sharpe 0.8 to 1.6, not to absurdity. That plausibility is why it survives
review.

**Signals.**
- `.resample(...)` producing an HTF series later joined back to the base frame
- `pd.merge_asof(...)` where the right frame is coarser
- a join on a truncated timestamp key (`floor('D')`, `to_period('W')`)

...with no shift applied to the HTF series before the merge.

**Fix.** Shift the HTF series by one full HTF period before merging, so row `t`
sees only the last *closed* higher-timeframe bar. Equivalently, key the merge on
the HTF bar's close time rather than its open time.

**Not a leak.** Control C04: `merge_asof(direction='backward')` with the HTF
series pre-shifted. `direction='backward'` alone does **not** make a merge
safe — it finds the most recent HTF row at or before `t`, which is the
currently-open bar whose value already reflects data through `t`.

**The helper is not the bug.** Freqtrade's `merge_informative_pair` advances the
informative row's date before joining, so row `t` sees only a closed bar —
measured, not assumed: swapping the hand-rolled join for the helper and changing
nothing else takes case 21 from a 95% to a 50% win rate over the same 20 trades.
Issue #11346 came from a strategy that *bypassed* it
with a hand-rolled `informative[informative['date'] <= row_time]` join. When you
see a higher-timeframe merge, ask whether the HTF stamp was advanced past its
own close, not whether a particular helper was called. Restoring the helper is
a fix, never a cause.

**Do not trust a truncation-based tool to clear this.** Freqtrade
lookahead-analysis reports no bias on case 21 (`eval/cases/htf_merge_11346`)
while the strategy wins 95% of its trades off the current week's close. The
reason is structural, not a tuning gap: it truncates its re-run to the entry
candle plus one base candle
(`optimize/analysis/lookahead.py:146-148`) and filters the informative series on
each candle's OPEN date (`data/dataprovider.py::historic_ohlcv`,
`data/converter/converter.py:159-160`). A weekly bar stamped Monday therefore
survives a truncation to Wednesday intact, already containing Friday's close, so
both runs read the same value and nothing differs. No open-date truncation can
expose a leak that lives inside one higher-timeframe candle — however the leak
got there. A "no bias" verdict from such a tool is not evidence of absence here;
only a before/after execution delta is.
