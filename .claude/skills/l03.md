# L03 — Same-bar execution mismatch

**Looks like.** A signal derived from bar `t`'s close (or high/low) drives a
position held *over* bar `t`. Canonical form: a missing `.shift(1)` between
signal and position. This is the 4.12 -> 0.34 example.

**Signals.** A boolean or numeric column derived from `close`/`high`/`low`, or
any indicator over them, assigned to a position and multiplied by a
*same-index* return. The signature is the **absence** of a shift between
derivation and use, so this rule is inherently over-productive — do not tune it
for precision, let the prover decide.

**Fix.** `.shift(1)` on the signal before it becomes a position.

    position = signal.shift(1).fillna(False).astype(int)  # decided at t-1, held over t

**Not a leak.** Control C01 is one token different — the shift is present.
This pair is where a static scanner and an execution prover disagree.

**Engine note.** Not directly expressible in `backtesting.py`, which fills at
the next open by default.
